# Arc 6 — Plot Context, Translation Priorities & Terminology

## Setting

The **Pleiades Watchtower** (Tháp Canh Pleiades) — a colossal tower rising from the middle of the Augria Sand Dunes (Cồn Cát Augria). The party came here to rescue Wilhelm van Astrea and others from the previous expedition who were unable to return. The tower is guarded by **Shaula**, who will only obey the successor of the Sage Flugel.

## The arc's emotional core

Subaru **loses his name** due to the tower's curse — no one can remember or record his name; written instances vanish. This is not a background detail: it is the arc's central emotional wound. Every scene touching on this is MAIN PLOT. Translate in full.

## The 5 Obstacles (Năm Chướng Ngại)

Introduced in Chapter 63. The tower has 5 obstacles that must be overcome to reach each floor and ultimately capture the tower. **All content directly related to the 5 obstacles is main plot — translate fully, no exceptions.**

| # | Vietnamese | Key chapter(s) |
|---|-----------|---------------|
| 1st | Chướng ngại thứ nhất | Earlier arc chapters |
| 2nd | Chướng ngại thứ hai | Chapter 64 |
| 3rd | Chướng ngại thứ ba | Mid-arc |
| 4th | Chướng ngại thứ tư | Mid-arc |
| 5th | Chướng ngại thứ năm | Chapter 65 references "The Second, The Fifth" |

The finale arc (Chapters ~79–90) is the tower conquest endgame — all of it is main plot.

---

## What to translate FULLY

- Any obstacle being introduced, attempted, or concluded
- Subaru's name disappearing or being recalled — every instance
- Beatrice and Subaru joint magic: **EMT** (Eternity Makes Time / Vĩnh Cửu Tạo Ra Thời Gian) and **EMM**
- Revelations about identity: who Shaula really is, who Flugel was, Louis Arneb as Archbishop of Gluttony
- Battle sequences that are part of the tower capture
- Any scene where characters reach an irreversible emotional decision
- The tower conquest finale (Chapters 85–90) — entirely main plot

## What to summarize (2–4 sentences, single Sentence block)

- Extended lore about Augria Sand Dunes history, ecology, failed expeditions
- Flashbacks to Subaru's life in Japan — UNLESS they bear directly on his current identity crisis or the name-loss theme
- Julius's explanatory monologues about the tower's past (keep the essential fact; cut the padding)
- Repeated internal monologue covering the same emotional ground the chapter already established
- World-building tangents: magic theory, witch beast biology, political context outside the tower
- Comedic tangents or extended jokes that have no plot consequence
